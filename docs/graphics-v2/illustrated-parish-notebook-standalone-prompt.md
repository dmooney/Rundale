# Illustrated Parish Notebook Standalone Prompt

This is a one-shot version of the illustrated parish notebook prompt. It does
not rely on seeing the earlier 7A or 7C reference images.

## Reference Elements Replaced With Text

- From the 7A conversation concept: selecting one NPC should open a
  conversation interface while the world remains visible; the UI should show
  the selected person's mood, trust, known facts, nearby witnesses, and
  compact talk/ask/help/observe actions plus a natural-language intent line.
- From the 7C roads and schedules concept: the camera should stay zoomed out
  enough to see a full isometric hub with roads, exits, destinations, villagers
  moving through the area, and the player's movement choices still available.
- From the illustrated notebook concept: the UI should feel like parchment,
  ink annotations, paper tabs, and notebook margins layered lightly over a
  hand-inked watercolor isometric scene.

## Standalone Prompt

```text
Use case: ui-mockup
Asset type: game interface concept art, 16:9 desktop screenshot mockup
Primary request: Create a graphical game interface concept for Rundale, an 1820 Irish parish living-world game. Show a wide isometric parish hub where the player has selected one villager for conversation, but the interface keeps the whole crossroads scene visible so movement, nearby witnesses, roads, and location context still matter. The concept name is "Illustrated Parish Notebook".

Scene/backdrop: A wide isometric hand-illustrated rural Irish parish crossroads in 1820. Include a small shop, chapel lane, bridge road, cottages, hedgerows, low stone walls, muddy tracks, puddles after rain, peat smoke, a few villagers walking or standing in natural clusters, and subtle road exits leading to other places. The camera is zoomed out like a full playable hub or navigation scene, not a close-up dialogue scene.

Core gameplay shown: The player has clicked or selected Roisin Connolly, a villager standing in the isometric scene. Roisin is highlighted with a hand-drawn circle and a small label. The UI shows that the player can talk to her while still seeing nearby witnesses, exits, and other villagers. The player can choose a compact action such as Talk, Ask, Help, or Observe, or type a natural-language intent into a slim input field.

Subject: A selected-NPC conversation interface presented through light parchment notebook UI, ink annotation overlays, small paper tabs, and stamped action buttons. The selected person panel should show mood, trust, known facts, and whether witnesses are nearby. Nearby people should appear as small sketched head icons or tiny list entries, not giant portraits.

Style/medium: Hand-inked watercolor isometric illustration with a historical notebook aesthetic. Fine sepia ink outlines, soft watercolor washes, cream parchment UI, marginal notes, paper texture, and elegant minimal game controls. The result should feel literary, handmade, historical, and playable. It should be original and not imitate any specific existing game.

Composition/framing: Full-screen 16:9 desktop game screenshot. The wide isometric village hub occupies most of the screen, roughly three quarters of the image. The bottom has a slim parchment conversation strip with action stamps and an intent line. The right side has a notebook page pinned open with mood, trust, known facts, and witness warning. The left side has small sketched head icons for nearby people. The top has a discreet handwritten-style ribbon for location, time, and weather. Keep the interface compact so the scene remains visible.

UI layout details: No giant character portraits and no modal dialogue box that covers the world. Action buttons should look like small ink stamps or paper tabs with icons for speech, question, helping hand, eye, and leaving. The selected NPC should be visually connected to the notebook panel with a light ink annotation or callout. Nearby witnesses should be marked by tiny eye symbols or subtle attention markers above villagers. Road exits can have small handwritten labels or signpost hints, but they should not dominate the conversation UI.

Lighting/mood: Cool gray afternoon after rain with warm paper texture in the interface. Reflective puddles, soft overcast light, quiet human activity, thoughtful and grounded atmosphere. The screen should feel intimate and literary while still zoomed out enough to be navigable.

Color palette: Watercolor greens, gray-blue rain, sepia ink, cream parchment, muted brick red, soft ochre, wet slate, dark peat shadows. The palette should be varied and gentle, not monochrome or overly saturated.

Text (verbatim, keep sparse and legible): "RUNDALE"; "Illustrated Parish Notebook"; "Crossroads"; "Afternoon clearing"; "Roisin Connolly"; "wary"; "Trust"; "She knows"; "cart delayed"; "flour is short"; "Witnesses nearby"; "Talk"; "Ask"; "Help"; "Observe"; "Intent"; "ask Roisin what she saw".

How it plays visually: The player explores a watercolor isometric parish hub, selects a villager in the scene, reads notebook-style marginal notes about mood and knowledge, notices nearby witnesses, chooses a stamped action or writes an intent, and the notebook updates with remembered facts while the village remains visible and traversable.

Constraints: no fantasy magic, no combat HUD, no weapons focus, no close-up camera, no modern city, no modern clothing, no phones, no exact imitation of any existing game UI, no web landing page, no huge tutorial text, no decorative gradient blobs, no stock photo, no watermark.
```
