# CA4 Grove Legend Input And Supplemental Hints

## Actual Legend

The actual legend is the OS 6-inch map key reference plate:

`docs/graphics-v2/web-references/os-6inch-map-key/os-6inch-map-key-reference-sheet.png`

Use that image as the legend/reference plate. Do not replace it with a
newly invented text legend. The notes below are supplemental interpretation
hints for reading the source crop against that plate.

## Supplemental Interpretation Hints

- Puffy circular tree symbols are deciduous trees.
- Tight triangular tree symbols are coniferous trees.
- Mixed tree symbols indicate mixed planting, not a separate structure class.
- Orchard or planted-enclosure symbols may combine boundary lines, internal
  rows or beds, and tree marks.
- Dark or hatched rectangular footprints are probable roofed structures.
  Preserve their count, orientation, and relative spacing.
- A broad pale corridor bounded by two road-edge lines is a physical road,
  lane, or cart road.
- If a bold dotted administrative boundary runs between two parallel road-edge
  lines, the physical feature is still the road. Ignore the dotted centerline as
  terrain; do not render it as stones, fence posts, or a second path.
- Paired light dashed linework corresponds to an unfenced road/path/track
  symbol. Mark it only where the paired corridor is visible; do not invent an
  extension past the evidence in the source crop.
- Single solid field or enclosure lines are boundaries, not walkable routes.
  Render them according to local material evidence, usually hedge, bank, ditch,
  fence, or occasional rough stone/stone-faced bank rather than uniform masonry.
- Dotted or pecked survey/administrative boundaries outside a road corridor are
  non-physical by default unless corroborated by another physical symbol.
- Dense trees with irregular small marks can indicate scrub, furze/whins,
  rough pasture, overgrown ditch, hedgerow, or mixed rough vegetation. Do not
  classify these as buildings without clear roof footprints.
- Stippled paper texture and ordinary field grain are not crop rows, walls,
  buildings, or paths without stronger matching symbols.
- Printed map labels and place names are map text only, not in-world signs or
  features.
- Water, marsh, bog, springs, wells, quarries, gravel pits, bridges, cliffs,
  slopes, rock, rough pasture, brushwood, furze/whins, and cropping rock should
  be marked only when their matching legend symbols are visible.
