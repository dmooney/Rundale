# Idea R Beechwood Close-Crop Notebook Style Report

## Source/topology fidelity

Good local fidelity. The result keeps the Beechwood main building cluster, enclosed courtyard, diagonal adjacent road, right-side planted garden/enclosure, nearby stone walls, gates, and tree masses as the visible play space. It avoids rendering the whole Cycle Q survey plate, and I do not see added water, bridges, church, graveyard, labels, people, animals, carts, smoke, or copied map text. The southern/smaller outbuildings are compressed toward the lower edge but still read as adjacent local context rather than a separate survey scene.

## Camera/scale result

The close crop worked. Doors, wall faces, thresholds, roof hatching, muddy road edges, courtyard paving, garden rows, shrubs, and stone walls are large enough for gameplay staging. The camera is a playable oblique view with substantial facades, though still slightly higher and more refined-isometric than the original notebook camera.

## Art-style match to original notebook sample and Grove R

This is close to Grove R in scale and finish: readable buildings, dark broken roof hatching, stone-wall texture, mottled olive fields, ochre road scumbling, and sepia ink contours. Compared with the original notebook sample, it is still cleaner and more systematized; roof tiles, wall stones, and garden rows are more regular than the rough human sketch/wash target. It lands nearer Grove R than the notebook source, but in the desired direction.

## Problems/risks

Some garden rows and stone-wall courses remain too orderly, so the image could still read as a polished isometric game plate rather than fully notebook-handmade. The main building cluster may be a little too architecturally crisp, and the oblique camera is not quite as low as the style target. The method still depends on visual inspection to catch any accidental invented building edges or over-literal physical walls.

## Generalizability

Promising. The Cycle Q + cleaned control + oblique pitch cue method appears generalizable for close playable crops around building clusters, especially when the desired local subset is already clear. For batch use, it should keep explicit anti-leakage constraints and likely add stronger roughness/irregularity language or post-selection scoring for wall/garden regularity.
