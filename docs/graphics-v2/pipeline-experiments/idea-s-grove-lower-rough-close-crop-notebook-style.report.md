# Idea S Grove Lower Rough Close Crop Notebook Style

- Topology preservation vs Cycle R/M/Q: strong. The plate keeps Cycle R's close playable frame, the central building/yard/garden cluster, the right-side barn, left outbuilding, lower road/wall edge, and upper garden enclosure relationships. It remains consistent with Cycle M's authoritative garden quadrants and main building cluster, while inheriting Cycle R's close crop rather than Cycle Q's wide survey view.
- Camera/scale vs Cycle R and original notebook: only a mild improvement. Facades, doorways, wall faces, and the barn opening read slightly more assertively than Cycle R, but the camera still feels closer to R's elevated oblique game plate than to the original notebook's lower, denser parish scene. Scale is good for the playable crop.
- Art-style roughness match: improved but incomplete. Roads, grass, garden beds, and limewash have more muddy wash, tooth, stains, and broken ink than Cycle R. Roofs and stone walls are still too regular in places: the roof slate pattern remains grid-like, and many boundary stones still read as tidy repeated units.
- New semantic/layout leaks: no major forbidden leaks seen. No UI, labels, visible text, people, animals, carts, water, bridges, churches, graveyards, shops, smoke, fog, or chimneys. The lower-right wall gains a small fence/gate-like detail that may be a new micro-layout element not clearly present in Cycle R/M.
- Problems/risks: the model mostly re-rendered Cycle R with rougher surface treatment instead of making a decisive camera/style shift. Garden rows are still somewhat systematic, roof hatching remains polished, and the new lower-right gate/fence detail should be checked before treating this as topology-clean.
- Better than Cycle R: marginally, for notebook roughness and material texture. Not decisively better overall; Cycle R is slightly cleaner as a topology baseline, while Cycle S is the better research sample if the priority is hand-made surface character.
