# Idea V Beechwood Tight Control Thatched Render Prompt

Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: topology-locked tight close-crop low 3/4 orthographic isomorphic game environment background plate, native 16:9 desktop, no UI

Input images and roles:
Image 1: tight top-down topology control crop for the target location. This is the primary layout authority for footprints, walls, roads, gates, enclosed yards, garden/enclosure boundaries, tree masses, and feature continuity. It is not a style reference and not an architectural-fidelity reference.
Image 2: deterministic oblique warp of Image 1. Camera-pitch cue only; do not copy beige margins, empty borders, strip artifacts, or flattened survey-map feeling.
Image 3: wider cleaned top-down control for the same target location. Secondary topology evidence if Image 1 edge context is ambiguous.
Image 4: original historic map crop. Source evidence only; use it to avoid invented features and to keep the result honest to the map symbols.
Image 5: cleaned low-camera slate/limewash building crop with clear doorway. Style, facade-height, threshold, wall, ink, watercolor, and camera reference only.
Image 6: cleaned single-house thatched/no-chimney crop with one clear doorway. Style, thatch, no-chimney, doorway, threshold, roughness, and camera reference only.
Image 7: original illustrated parish notebook scene. Broad medium/mood reference only; do not copy its UI, church, bridge, river, people, animals, carts, labels, signs, smoke, or specific landmarks.
Images 8-9: material swatches only for field walls, roofs, rough ground, limewash, grass, and ink/watercolor texture.

Primary request:
Create one 16:9 background plate for the target crop. It should look like a hand-drawn illustrated parish notebook game background: ink outlines, muted watercolor washes, rough stone, muddy road edges, patchy grass, hand-built irregularity, and readable human-scale facades. Preserve the target topology from Image 1 while translating it into a lower 3/4 orthographic isomorphic game plate.

Critical topology rule:
Image 1 is the layout authority. Preserve the exact relationships of connected or attached building footprints shown there. If buildings touch or form one compound in Image 1, keep them touching or visibly attached in the final. Do not dissolve a connected compound into separate detached cottages. Do not rearrange the yard/courtyard/enclosure that sits inside or beside that compound. Do not add extra buildings, churches, sheds, paths, roads, water, bridges, gardens, labels, signs, people, animals, carts, smoke, or chimneys.

Camera and scale:
Use a low 3/4 orthographic isomorphic camera suitable for characters walking over the plate. North stays up in the composition: features toward the top of the source crop remain toward the top of the final plate. The camera should be lower than a survey-map view: walls and building facades must have visible vertical faces; doors should be readable; roof planes should not dominate the whole image. Keep consistent orthographic scale with no fisheye, no perspective vanishing point, no extreme cinematic horizon, and no UI.

Building translation:
Translate source-map building footprints into humble early-19th-century rural Irish structures. Prefer rough limewashed stone walls, patched plaster, irregular hand-built geometry, low eaves, rough thatch where appropriate, and simple dark door openings. Do not use grand estate architecture, formal mansion geometry, modern cottages, fantasy medieval buildings, or polished town architecture. Roof material may be thatch or rough slate if required by the visual translation, but the footprint and adjacency from Image 1 must not change. No visible chimneys and no smoke.

Door/facade rule:
Every visible playable building facade that faces a road, yard, lane, or courtyard must have a readable door or threshold unless that facade is truly cropped off-frame or fully occluded by another source-faithful object. A foreground or edge building with no readable door is a failure. Doors should look like simple dark timber openings set into limewashed or stone walls, with a small worn threshold or muddy approach.

Map-symbol discipline:
Treat historic map text, letters, numbers, labels, administrative dotted boundaries, survey dots, hatching, and paper texture as source artifacts, not physical content, unless corroborated by real-world symbols such as roads, tree clusters, walls, hedge rows, building footprints, garden plots, watercourses, or gates. Do not turn administrative dot chains into hedges, bushes, fences, or paths. If a real road or wall exits the crop, continue it naturally off-frame; do not make it stop abruptly.

Composition:
Tight playable crop, human-scale detail. Center the key building compound and its immediate yard/garden/road context. Include only the local context visible in the control crop: nearby walls, road edges, enclosed garden/field texture, and tree/hedge masses where they exist. Leave enough walkable ground around doors and yard entries for small character sprites. No decorative vignette, no paper border, no text labels, no UI panels.

Style:
Match the original illustrated parish notebook look: fine black/brown ink linework, uneven hand watercolor, soft desaturated greens and ochres, slate-gray or straw-brown roof texture, off-white limewash, damp earth, rough stone walls, small imperfect strokes, and varied natural texture. Avoid clean CAD geometry, tile-game regularity, glossy concept art, photorealism, anime, 3D render, vector art, perfect grids, sharp digital outlines, and overly polished roofs.

Final output:
One native 16:9 PNG background plate, no UI, no labels, no border, no characters, no props that imply gameplay events. The result should be ready to audit by overlaying against the source/control crop for topology.
